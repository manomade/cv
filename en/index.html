Identity & Positioning

Profile

Media producer and creative technologist. More than fifteen years in media design [ web · video · graphics ] alongside fine arts and curatorial practices. He owns the chain from concept and art direction through pipeline, installation and delivery, and works as an artist and curator on the same field. Most recently audiovisual production lead on Tecnologia [in]visible for the studio Gracias Grecia, a public exhibition at Palau Robert commissioned by Mobile World Capital Barcelona and the Generalitat de Catalunya.

Mano Leyrado (Buenos Aires, 1987) first studies in Art Curating and Cultural Management (CIC, Buenos Aires, 2018), then earned his Degree in Fine Arts with honors in Sculpture from Weißensee KHB, Berlin (2020 to 2025) and completed his Master there in 2026. He co-founded the art space CPU at Palacio Barolo, Buenos Aires. In 2026 his video Elenchus was acquired by the Belvedere's Ursula Blickle Video Archiv, Vienna; he was 1st runner-up for the Beyond Future Art Prize 2025.

Three practices, one person

Media design, fine arts and curating operate as a single practice. That means full ownership of a project from concept and critical framing through visual identity, technical production and communication: writing the curatorial text, building the platform, directing the shoot, setting up a generative pipeline, and knowing which of those a given project actually needs.

Generative AI runs through the work in two directions at once. He builds production pipelines with it, ComfyUI depth maps that end in cast wax. He also makes work that examines the sociopolitical implications of technology.

Artistic Practice

Statement

Mano works across sculpture, video installation and digital media. His practice treats materiality as a form of argument: wax figures that consume themselves over the duration of an exhibition, archaeological reliefs cast from obsolete technology, a philosophical dialogue with an AI agent that collapses into absurdity. Each material and medium is chosen to carry the concept, not to illustrate it.

Recurring concerns include the transformation of labour under platform economies, the construction and mediation of nature, and the tension between physical presence and digital abstraction. The work does not propose answers; it stages contemporary conditions seeking to expose their paradoxes.

Wax and stone dust, site-specific sculpture · unit 67 × 35 × 3.5 cm
Tensions between body, labour and technology in a present defined by hyperconnectivity and accelerated obsolescence. Curatorial Work

Approach

Curation as a laboratory: spaces conceived to transcend established notions of aesthetic discourse and institutional practice, creating conditions for experimentation rather than presenting conclusions. Recurring interests are the intersection of contemporary practice and digital culture, relational and participatory formats, the integration of curatorial and artistic research, and community-based platforms, with particular experience in Latin American artists navigating European institutional landscapes.
